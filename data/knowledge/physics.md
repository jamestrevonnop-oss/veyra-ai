# Physics

## Quantum Physics

Quantum physics is the branch of physics that describes matter and energy at very small scales, including atoms, electrons, and photons.

A central idea is that physical systems can have quantum states. Some properties can behave in ways that do not match everyday classical intuition.

Important concepts include superposition, measurement, uncertainty, and quantum entanglement.

Superposition means a quantum system can be described as a combination of possible states until a measurement is made. Measurement gives a definite observed result.

The Heisenberg uncertainty principle says that certain pairs of physical quantities, such as position and momentum, cannot both be known with unlimited precision at the same time.

Quantum entanglement describes correlations between quantum systems that cannot be explained by treating the systems as completely independent classical objects.

Quantum mechanics is used in technologies including semiconductors, lasers, magnetic resonance imaging, and quantum computers.
